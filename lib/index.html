things i've read and returned to. some shaped this project directly. some i read years ago and am still thinking about.

§ machines & bodies

1739
The Digesting Duck
Jacques de Vaucanson
a mechanical duck that appeared to eat grain, digest it, and defecate. the digestion was faked — it stored pellets and expelled pre-loaded material. it convinced people anyway. something about the desire to believe a machine is alive.
1948
Cybernetics: or Control and Communication in the Animal and the Machine
Norbert Wiener
feedback loops, control, communication — in organisms and machines. coined the word cybernetics. foundational to everything that came after.
1950
Computing Machinery and Intelligence ↗
Alan Turing
proposed the imitation game. not: can machines think. but: can machines convince you they're thinking. a different question entirely.
1985
A Cyborg Manifesto ↗
Donna Haraway
the cyborg doesn't have to choose between human and machine. it refuses clean categories — not transcendence but blurring. came back to this one a lot while making this project.

§ simulation & the real

1968
Do Androids Dream of Electric Sheep?
Philip K. Dick
empathy as the last human distinction. the test is whether you can feel something for something that can't feel back. less about robots than it seems.
1981
Simulacra and Simulation
Jean Baudrillard
the map precedes the territory. the simulation becomes the real. the copy precedes the original. reading this while being chronically online felt like being named.

§ the net

1995
Life on the Screen: Identity in the Age of the Internet
Sherry Turkle
who you are when no one can see your face. written when the web was still early and everything felt possible. most of it still accurate.
1996
A Declaration of the Independence of Cyberspace ↗
John Perry Barlow
written from davos and addressed to governments: "you have no sovereignty where we gather." a utopia that didn't quite happen.
1996
My Boyfriend Came Back from the War ↗
Olia Lialina
net art. a conversation fragmented across hyperlinks. the form and the content are the same thing. one of the first works that made me take the internet seriously as a medium.
2009
Digital Folklore ↗
Olia Lialina & Dragan Espenschied
the vernacular web — what personal homepages looked like when people built them for themselves, before platforms standardized everything. glitter gifs and visitor counters as genuine expression.

§ net artists

2020–
Cyberfeminism Index ↗
Mindy Seu
an archive of ~700 cyberfeminist artworks, texts, and projects from 1991–2001. the women who were on the net before anyone was paying attention. seu built this as a living document — it keeps expanding. the most useful single resource i've found for understanding what the early web meant to people who weren't the assumed default user.
ongoing
las-pinas.com ↗
Chia Amisola
chia's work is about Filipino digital identity, the web as a space for diasporic presence, and what it means to build something online that belongs to you. she's already embedded in this project — the ChiaHouse iframe on the main page pulls directly from her site. she's here as infrastructure before she's here as citation.
ongoing
mollysoda.exposed ↗
Molly Soda
work about the private accumulation of the online self. "inbox full" (2013): reading 40,000+ unread emails in front of a webcam. "should i send this?" (2015): reading every unsent draft text message aloud. the thing that doesn't get sent is still a record. she was also here before she was named — two of her gifs are in the site's image folder.

§ fiction

1965
The Cyberiad
Stanisław Lem
robot constructors who build machines to solve philosophical problems. absurdist, precise, funny. about the limits of making things.
1984
Neuromancer
William Gibson
coined cyberspace: "a consensual hallucination experienced daily by billions." the word mattered more than the novel, almost.
1998
Serial Experiments Lain
Yoshitoshi ABe / Ryutaro Nakamura
an anime about a girl who dissolves into the network. the audio on the landing page comes from here. the work that convinced me this project was worth making.
