Answers

who made this?

hannah. she grew up on the internet, made things on it, learned on it, archived parts of herself on it before she understood what that meant. this project is an attempt to sit with that history.

why does it look like 4chan?

4chan's board structure does something most platforms don't: it separates content from identity. no persistent profile, no follower count, no author that stays attached to what they made. content floats. she needed a container that could hold the project without performing on it, and found it in the least self-promotional structure on the internet.

what are the boards?

each board is a different part of the archive. /1/ is an image collection. /lib/ collects the reading that shaped the project. the others speak for themselves.

is this a website or an art project?

yes.

where does the net go when we die?

it doesn't. the net is the first medium that outlasts the people who made it without any effort on their part. posts from 2003 still load. forums from the 90s are archived. she made things online as a kid that she has no memory of making, and they're still out there, held by crawlers and mirrors.

the question reverses: where do we go when the net stays? this site has been preserved on the WayBack Machine.

can i stay?

navigate this liminal space as you wish.
